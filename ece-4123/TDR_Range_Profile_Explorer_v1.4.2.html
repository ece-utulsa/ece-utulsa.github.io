Pulse out, echoes back
t = 0 ns

The instrument launches a short pulse (red) down the line; its length v/B is set by the bandwidth. Each scatterer sends back an echo of strength ρ (blue). Where echoes overlap they add as phasors. The echoes arriving back at the instrument, plotted against R = v·t/2, are the range profile below. A label ending “(+n)” stands for n more returns too close to label separately.

Range profile

Model: single bounce. Each return is drawn as if nothing ahead of it reflected: a reflector ahead really lets through only (1 − ρ²) of a later echo, out and back, and multiple-reflection ghosts are left out.

Raw magnitude of the measured frequency response |S(f)| the VNA samples across the sweep — the range profile above is its inverse transform.

Bandwidth sets resolution Δr = v/(2B), where v = vp = VoP·c is the velocity on the line. Two returns closer than Δr show as one peak, lower and broader as their phase difference grows, unless they are near opposite in phase: then they show a dip and two lobes at false ranges. Whether a close pair separates depends on relative phase as well as on bandwidth. Center frequency sets the wavelength λc. The echo travels the extra distance twice, so at fc the farther of two returns lags by 4πΔR/λc; their relative phase is θ2 − θ1 − 4πΔR/λc. Moving one return by λc/4 swings the pair from reinforcing to cancelling (λc/2 is a full cycle). Phase θ on each return (set in the list) controls how neighbours combine — equal phase reinforces, opposite (180°) cancels. Points set the frequency step Δf = B/(N−1) and the unambiguous range Runamb = v(N−1)/(2B); too few points for the chosen bandwidth makes distant returns alias. Window trades a sharp peak with ringing against a rounded peak with none. Gate keeps only returns inside a chosen range, like time-gating in ADS.
