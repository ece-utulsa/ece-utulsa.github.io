Pulse out, echoes back
t = 0 ns

The instrument launches a short pulse (red) down the line; its length v/B is set by the bandwidth. Each scatterer sends back an echo of strength ρ (blue). Where echoes overlap they add as phasors. The echoes arriving back at the instrument, plotted against R = v·t/2, are the range profile below.

Raw magnitude of the measured frequency response |S(f)| the VNA samples across the sweep — the range profile above is its inverse transform.

Bandwidth sets resolution Δr = c/(2B). Two returns closer than Δr merge into one peak only when they are nearly in phase; out of phase they show a dip and two lobes at false ranges, so whether a close pair separates depends on their relative phase as well as on bandwidth. Center frequency sets the wavelength λc. The echo travels the extra distance twice, so the relative phase is 4πΔR/λc: moving one of two nearby returns by λc/4 swings the pair from reinforcing to cancelling (λc/2 is a full cycle). Phase θ on each return (set in the list) controls how neighbours combine — equal phase reinforces, opposite (180°) cancels. Points set the frequency step Δf = B/(N−1) and the unambiguous range Runamb = c(N−1)/(2B); too few points for the chosen bandwidth makes distant returns alias. Window trades a sharp peak with ringing against a rounded peak with none. Gate keeps only returns inside a chosen range, like time-gating in ADS.
