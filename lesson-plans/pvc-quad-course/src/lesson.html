PVC Quad Course

A drone has to take off, grab a 4-inch cube, fly through four PVC gates, drop the cube in a bin and land, all inside a netted cage 10 ft wide and 20 ft tall. Find out how four spinning props steer a drone, plan its flight on a map, program it with blocks, watch an autopilot work out its own route, fly it yourself, take on the challenges, and then design a course of your own.

Learning objectives

By the end, students can:

  1. Explain how changing the speed of four props makes a drone climb, tilt, move and turn.
  2. Describe a point in 3D space with (x, y, height) coordinates.
  3. Plan a flight path as a list of waypoints and check it for collisions.
  4. Write a short program with a loop, a sensor reading and a decision (if / else).
  5. Describe in their own words how an autopilot finds a safe route (map the obstacles, search, straighten).
  6. Compare manual, programmed and autonomous flight.

The activities

  • §01 Meet the drone: a 3D drone with four motor sliders and thrust arrows. Presets, "what happens?" and a 3-question check.
  • §02 Plan a path: click a map of the cage to place waypoints, set heights in the side view, pre-flight check, then fly it.
  • §03 Program it: snap together blocks (take off, fly to, move, fly through gate, grab, repeat, repeat until, if / else, sensors); the running block lights up; see the same program as Python-style code.
  • §04 Autopilot: watch the route search spread through the cage, step by step; then fly the full mission and move the cube.
  • §05 Fly it yourself: keyboard or on-screen pad, follow camera and drone-cam inset.
  • §06 Challenges: five scored levels, any flying mode, stars and a best score.
  • §07 Design a course (extension): move the pad, cube and bin, add gates, hoops and poles, check the course, share it as a code.

Key terms

Thrust
The upward push from a spinning prop.
Pitch / roll / yaw
Tilting nose up or down / tilting left or right / turning to face a new direction.
Waypoint
A point in space the drone flies to; a flight path is a list of waypoints.
Coordinates
(x, y, height) in feet: x east, y north, height above the floor. The cage centre is x = 0, y = 0.
Program
A list of instructions the drone follows one at a time. A loop repeats instructions; an if chooses between them.
Sensor
A part that measures something; here, the distance to the nearest object in five directions, and a camera that looks for the cube.
Autonomous
The drone decides its own route from its map and sensors.
Clearance
The space kept between the drone and an obstacle. Here the drone needs about 0.5 ft all round.

01Meet the drone

A quadcopter has four props. Each one pushes air down and the drone up; that push is thrust. There is no rudder and no wing flap: the flight controller steers only by making some props spin faster than others. Drag the four sliders, or try a preset, and watch the arrows and the drone.

What happens
Up and down: all four props together. More thrust than the drone's weight and it climbs; exactly its weight and it hovers. Moving: the drone tilts, and the tilted thrust pushes it sideways. Faster back props tip the nose down, so it flies forward.
Turning: the props spin in opposite directions in pairs (front-left with back-right, front-right with back-left). Each prop twists the body the other way. Speed up one pair and slow the other, and the twists no longer cancel: the drone turns on the spot.

02Plan a path

Every spot in the cage has an address: x (feet east of the centre), y (feet north) and height (feet above the floor). The floor runs from −5 to 5 in x and y, and the drone must stay 0.5 ft away from the nets. A flight path is a list of these points, called waypoints, flown in straight lines.

How to plan: click the map to add a waypoint, drag one to move it, and drag it up or down in the side view to set its height. Tag one waypoint pick (grab the cube), one drop (over the bin) and the last land. The pre-flight check lists every leg that hits something. Then press Launch on the flight deck below.

      03Program it

      A path is a list of places. A program is a list of instructions, and it can do things a path cannot: repeat steps, read a sensor and decide what to do next. Snap blocks together, then press Run program. The drone does one block at a time, and the block it is doing lights up yellow.

      How: click a block in the coloured list to add it to your program at the orange marker. Click a gap in your program to move the marker there. You can also drag blocks in. On each block, ▲ ▼ move it, ✕ deletes it and ⠿ drags it. Positions and heights are in feet, as on the map in §02.

      05Fly it yourself
      
  
      Now you are the flight controller. Press Launch, then climb with R (or UP on the pad). W S fly forward and back, A D left and right, Q E turn, F down. Hover with the claw just above the cube and press G to grab; press G again over the bin to drop. Click the 3D view first so it hears the keys.
      
  
      The Follow camera stays behind the drone, and the drone cam inset shows what its own camera sees. Depth is hard to judge from one camera: switch to Top to line up over the cube.

        08Exit ticket
        
  
        Answer on paper.
        
  
          
    
        1. Your drone is hovering. Which props must speed up, and which slow down, to make it fly backward? Why?
          
      
          The front props speed up and the back props slow down. The nose tips up, the thrust tilts backward, and the drone moves backward (total thrust stays the same, so it keeps its height).
          2. 
    
        2. Write the waypoint for a point 2 ft west of the centre, 3 ft north of it and 6 ft above the floor.
          
      
          (x, y, height) = (−2, 3, 6).
          3. 
    
        3. The cube was moved. Explain why the autopilot still picked it up but your saved path missed it.
          
      
          A saved path is a fixed list of points, so the drone flies to where the cube used to be. The autopilot finds the cube with its camera and plans a new route around the pipes from where the cube actually is.
          4. 
    
        4. A program says repeat until down sensor < 0.4 ft: move down 0.25 ft. What does the drone do? Why is that better than one block that says move down 2.5 ft?
          
      
          It steps down a quarter of a foot at a time, checking the sensor before each step, and stops when something is less than 0.4 ft below it. It works from any starting height and stops above whatever is there (the cube, the floor, the bin); a fixed 2.5 ft move only works from exactly the right height and could hit the cube or the floor.
