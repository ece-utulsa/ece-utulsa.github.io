Voltage Regulation

ECE 3033 · Lecture 3 · v1.0.0
Model

Three-phase, Y-connected synchronous generator: 2.5 MVA, 4160 V, 60 Hz, RA = 0.12 Ω and XS = 6.2 Ω per phase. Vφ = 2,402 V at full load; rated IA = 347.0 A.

Per phase, EA = Vφ + IA(RA + jXS), with Vφ as the reference. The field is set so that Vφ = 2,402 V at full load and is then held fixed, so |EA| does not change when the load is removed (no saturation, speed constant). At no load IA = 0 and Vφ = EA.

VR = (|EA| − |Vφ|) / |Vφ| × 100 %. In the phasor diagram IA is drawn on its own scale: its direction is exact, its length is not comparable with the voltages.

On slide 1 the voltmeter moves to its new value with a short display lag; the electrical transient of a real machine is not modelled.

1 of 3 · Voltage regulation

Remove the load and the terminal voltage moves to EA.

With the field fixed, VR measures how far the voltage moves between full load and no load.

At no load IA = 0, so nothing drops across RA or XS: Vnl = EA.

2 of 3 · Power factor

Power factor decides the voltage regulation.

Lagging loads need a larger EA; a leading load can need less than Vφ.

3 of 3 · Key points and your turn

Three results to keep, then one to work out.

VR is the rise in terminal voltage when full load is removed, field fixed.

  • Lagging load: large positive VR.
  • Unity power factor: smaller positive VR.
  • Leading load: VR can be negative; the voltage falls.
