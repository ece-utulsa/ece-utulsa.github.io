Paralleling a Synchronous Generator

ECE 3033 · Lecture 1 · v2.0.0
Model

Incoming machine G3: 25 MVA, 13.8 kV, 4 poles, XS = 1.0 pu = 7.62 Ω, rated current 1046 A. The bus is infinite: 13.8 kV, 60.00 Hz, sequence ABC. Vg ∝ IF × speed (open circuit, no saturation).

Surge at the close ≈ |ΔV|/XS on the worst breaker pole, using the steady-state XS; the real first-cycle surge is larger (subtransient reactance X″d). Up to 0.20 × rated: smooth. Up to 0.60 × rated: a jolt. Above that, or wrong sequence, or |slip| > 0.5 Hz (cannot pull into step): a trip.

After joining, the governor's 4 % droop gives P = sP(fnl − fsys), sP = 10.4 MW/Hz; fnl is the governor set point and fsys = 60.00 Hz. Ready-to-close tolerances: |ΔV| ≤ 5 %, |Δθ| ≤ 10°, 0 < slip ≤ 0.20 Hz.

Lamps: dark below 20 % of 2V (a dim red ember from 5 %), then brighter up to 2V. Run-up and field build-up are shown faster than a real machine. Slide 1 is a schematic plant, not to scale: five 60 MW units, hourly load joined by straight lines, one unit switched each time the load crosses a multiple of 60 MW.

1 of 8 · Why parallel

Units come on and off as the load changes.

Paralleling connects a generator to a running system. At the instant its breaker closes, four conditions must hold:

  1. same voltage (slide 3)
  2. same phase sequence (slide 4)
  3. same phase angle (slide 5)
  4. frequency slightly higher (slide 6)

Infinite bus: so large that its voltage and frequency don't change, whatever one machine does.

Unloading: lower the governor setting to about 0 MW and trim the field to about 0 MVAR, then open the breaker.

3 of 8 · Condition 1: voltage

Unequal voltages: the lamps never go fully dark.

Closing with Vg ≠ Vbus drives a reactive current through the machine.

I≈|Vbus−Vg|XS
Q≈3Vbus(Vg−Vbus)XS

Vg = 0.85 Vbus here; the other three conditions are met.

4 of 8 · Condition 2: phase sequence

Wrong sequence: the lamps take turns, and closing is a short circuit.

Swapping any two leads reverses the sequence; never close until the lamps blink together.

Vpole,B=Vpole,C=3V

across breaker poles B and C when lamp A is dark (sequence ACB); V = 7.97 kV per phase.

Cross-connected lamps (sidebar): with the right sequence the light rotates; close when lamp A is dark.

5 of 8 · Condition 3: phase angle

Out of phase: the voltage across the breaker drives a surge.

Close only at Δθ ≈ 0: lamps dark, synchroscope pointer at 12 o'clock.

|ΔV|=2Vsin(Δθ2)

Voltage, sequence and frequency are right; only the closing angle is wrong.

6 of 8 · Condition 4: frequency

The lamps blink at the frequency difference; close slightly fast.

Slightly fast: it picks up load. Slow: it motors. Far off: it trips.

blinks/s=|fg−fbus|

Move the governor and count the dark moments. Each demo closes in phase.

Experiments

Six short investigations, about 40 minutes. Each ▶ Set up puts the Lab in the right state. Predict before you look. ▶ Listen reads the task aloud.

Experiment 1

Wrong phase sequence

Goal: see what the lamps do when two leads are swapped, and fix it.

  1. Predict: will lamps A, B, C blink together or one after another?
  2. Watch for 10 s. Then press ⇄ Swap two leads (b↔c).
  1. Prediction, then what you saw with ACB.
  2. Why are the lamps never all dark at the same time with ACB?
What you should see

With ACB the lamps take turns — the dark lamp moves A → C → B (or the reverse) and never all three go dark together, because phases B and C of the generator sit 120° away from the bus phases they face. Swapping the two leads back makes all three brighten and go dark together.

Experiment 2

Blink rate and frequency difference

Goal: show that the blink rate equals |fg − fbus|.

  1. Count dark moments in 10 s at each frequency; set it with the Governor slider and its − and + buttons.

  1. Predict first: how many blinks in 10 s at 0.20 Hz? Then explain the pattern. What do the two waves in the waveform panel do?
What you should see

5, 2 and 0.5 blinks in 10 s: blinks per second = |fg − fbus|. In the waveform panel the generator wave slides past the bus wave once for every blink. The phase difference does not set the rate; it sets how bright the lamps are at each instant.

Close at the wrong moment

Goal: compare the surge for a close at Δθ = 90°, 180° and ≈ 0°.

  1. Time is paused at the set angle. Press Close and read the surge meter. Then ↺ Reset and use the next set-up.

  1. Predict first: which angle gives the largest surge? Check with |ΔV| = 2V sin(Δθ/2).
What you should see

90°: |ΔV| = 11.27 kV, surge ≈ 1479 A (1.41 × rated) — the breaker closes, then trips. 180°: 15.93 kV, ≈ 2092 A (2.00 × rated) — trip. 5°: 0.70 kV, ≈ 91 A (0.09 × rated) — stays closed, IN STEP; equal frequencies here, so P ≈ 0.

Experiment 4

Unequal voltages

Goal: see why the lamps never go fully dark when Vg ≠ Vbus, then close anyway.

  1. The set-up puts the field low on purpose (Vg = 8.29 kV). Watch the lamps at their darkest; read lamp A's voltage then.
  2. Close when the lamps are darkest. Read the surge.
  1. Why do the lamps never go fully dark?
  2. What surge did you get, and which condition was missing?
What you should see

At Δθ = 0 the lamp still reads |Vbus − Vg| = 7.97 − 4.78 = 3.18 kV per phase, so the lamps stay dimly lit. Closing at Δθ ≈ 0 gives about 418 A (0.40 × rated): it joins with a jolt (amber tag). Condition 1, equal voltages, was missing.

Experiment 5

Close with the generator slow

Goal: find which way power flows after closing with fg below the bus.

  1. Watch the synchroscope direction. Close when the lamps are dark.
  1. Predict first: generating or motoring? Then read P after the close.
What you should see

The pointer turns counter-clockwise (SLOW). After the close the machine is pulled up to 60.00 Hz and runs as a motor: P = 10.4 × (59.90 − 60.00) ≈ −1.0 MW. That is why condition 4 asks for fg slightly above the bus.

Experiment 6

Your turn: bring G3 onto the bus

Goal: G3 is at rest with its breaker open, and two of its leads have been swapped. Find and fix the wrong sequence, meet all four conditions, and close on dark lamps.

  1. Run up, excite the field and raise Vg to 13.80 kV. Fix the sequence if the lamps take turns. Set fg slightly above 60 Hz, then close when the lamps are dark.
  2. The Lab shows the five steps; each ticks as you meet it (they tick here too).
  1. ○1 Run up to speed, field off.
  2. ○2 Field: Vg = Vbus (condition 1).
  3. ○3 Lamps take turns? Swap two leads (condition 2).
  4. ○4 fg slightly above fbus (condition 4).
  5. ○5 Close when lamps are dark (condition 3).
  1. Predict first: how will you know, before you close, that the sequence is wrong?
  2. After your clean close: what surge did you get, what P does G3 carry, and how would you make it carry more?
What you should see

With two leads swapped the lamps take turns: the dark lamp moves from one to the next and all three are never dark together, and the Ready-to-close checklist shows the sequence ✗. Swap two leads and they blink together. A close with the lamps dark, Vg = 13.80 kV and fg slightly high is clean: the surge is about 0 A (IN STEP) and all five steps are ✓. G3 then carries P = 10.4 × (fg − 60.00) MW, for example +0.5 MW at 60.05 Hz; raise the governor setting for more.
