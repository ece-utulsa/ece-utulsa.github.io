Load Sharing and Speed Droop

ECE 3033 · Lecture 4 · v1.2.0
Model

Three 100 MW units with 4 poles (shaft speed in r/min = 30 × frequency in Hz). G1: fnl = 61.0 Hz, SD = 3.0 %. G2: 61.5 Hz, 4.0 %. G3: 60.8 Hz, 2.5 %. Speed droop is SD = (nnl − nfl) / nfl × 100 %; speed and frequency are in proportion, so the same ratio holds in hertz.

Each unit follows a straight droop line: ffl = fnl / (1 + SD), sP = Pfl / (fnl − ffl) with Pfl = 100 MW, and P = sP(fnl − fsys). Units sharing a load run at one frequency, fsys = (Σ sPk fnl,k − Pload) / Σ sPk. On the infinite bus fsys = 60.00 Hz whatever the unit does.

Moving a set point (slides 2 and 4) slides the droop line parallel to itself: the unit keeps the slope sP it has at its starting set point. On slide 1 the slope follows the fnl and SD sliders.

Steady state only: the frequency and the powers jump to their new values; governor and rotor dynamics are not modelled. The lines are drawn past 100 MW and below 0 MW to show the trend; a unit there is flagged (above its rating, or motoring on reverse power). Losses and reactive power are left out. The block diagram on slide 1 is a schematic of the speed-control loop, not a model of its dynamics.

Numbers on screen are rounded (0.1 MW, 0.01 Hz, 0.1 MW/Hz, 0.1 %, 1 r/min); the model computes with the unrounded values, so a line worked by hand from the rounded numbers can differ in the last digit.

1 of 5 · Speed droop

More load, lower speed, on purpose.

Speed droop is the fall in prime-mover speed from no load to full load, as a percentage of the full-load speed.

The governor on the prime mover senses only speed. When load slows the shaft, it opens the valve in proportion to the fall. That proportion is the droop.

Small SD: flat line, many MW per Hz, a stiff unit.

2 of 5 · On the infinite bus

The bus fixes the frequency; the set point decides the power.

Raise fnl and the unit takes more load; the bus supplies the rest.

The bus holds 60.00 Hz whatever G1 does. G1's power is where its droop line crosses that frequency. Raising the set point slides the line up and the crossing to the right. The faint dashed line is where the line was.

The area load does not change G1's power: the bus takes up the difference, Pbus = Pload − PG1.

3 of 5 · Sharing a load

One frequency, three droop lines.

The frequency settles where the three units together carry the load. A load step is shared in proportion to sP.

No bus holds the frequency here. All three units turn at the same electrical speed, so one horizontal line cuts all three droop lines; each crossing is that unit's power.

4 of 5 · Moving a set point

Raise one set point: the frequency comes back.

Raise one set point: that unit takes more, the others less, and the frequency rises.

Droop alone never restores 60 Hz; an operator or automatic control moves a set point.

The load has not changed, so what one unit picks up the others give back. The faint dashed line is where the moved unit's line was.
