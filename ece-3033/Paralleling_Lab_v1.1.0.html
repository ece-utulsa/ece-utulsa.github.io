Paralleling a Synchronous Generator

ECE 3033 · v1.1.0

1 · WHY PARALLEL

Several generators share one varying load.

Units come on and off the bus as the load changes, and back each other up.

The live scene: an incoming 4-pole generator, breaker open, beside a 60 Hz, 13.8 kV running system.

Unequal voltages: lamps never fully dark.

4 · CONDITION 2: PHASE SEQUENCE

Sequence is the order in which a, b, c pass a fixed line.

Swapping any two phases reverses the sequence.

ABC: va∠0°,vb∠−120°,vc∠+120°ACB: va∠0°,vb∠+120°,vc∠−120°

Phasors below the scene (shown slowed). The rotation meter and a test motor show the generator's sequence: the motor runs backwards in ACB. Magnitudes and frequency are equal, so only the sequence differs.

5 · CONDITION 2: THE LAMP SIGNATURE

Lamps together: right sequence. Lamps take turns: wrong.

In ACB the dark spot rotates; the three lamps are never dark together.

Vlamp,k=|Vbus,k−Vg,k|blinks per second=|fg−fbus|

The darkest lamp's label is underlined. Alternative textbook method, cross-connected lamps (two bright, one dark): with the right sequence the light rotates, its direction shows fast or slow, and you close when the straight lamp A is dark.

6 · CONDITION 2 NOT MET: CLOSING IN ACB

Wrong sequence: closing is a short circuit.

Even with lamp A dark, phases B and C see √3 V across the breaker.

Vlamp,k=|Vbus,k−Vg,k|Isurge≈|Vbus−Vg|XS

Never an achievable synchronisation: with ACB no instant has all three breaker voltages at zero.

7 · CONDITION 3 NOT MET: PHASE ANGLE

Out of phase: surge and torque shock — worst at 180°.

The surge grows with ΔV = 2V sin(Δθ/2): 1.41 × rated at 90°, 2 × at 180°.

Vlamp=2Vsin(Δθ2)(equal magnitudes)Isurge≈|Vbus−Vg|XS

Voltages, sequence and frequency are right; only the closing angle is wrong.

8 · CONDITION 4 NOT MET: FREQUENCY

Slightly higher (0.05–0.2 Hz) so it comes on generating.

Far off: no pull-in. Slightly low: it joins and motors.

blinks per second=|fg−fbus|P=sP(fnl−fsys)

Voltages, sequence and angle are right at the moment of closing; only the frequency is wrong.

9 · ALL FOUR MET

In step: it joins smoothly and picks up a little power.

The waves lock together; the governor set point decides P.

P=sP(fnl−fsys)SD=nnl−nflnfl

sP follows from the governor droop: 4 % droop on 25 MW gives sP = 25 / (0.04 × 60) = 10.4 MW/Hz.

10 · THE PROCEDURE

Five steps, each one meeting a condition.

Speed, field, sequence, frequency, then close at the dark instant.

fe=nmP120Vlamp=|Vbus−Vg|

  1. Run up to speed, field off — fg near fbus.
  2. Raise IF until Vg = Vbus — condition 1.
  3. Check the lamps take no turns — condition 2.
  4. Set fg slightly above fbus — condition 4.
  5. Close when the lamps are dark, pointer at 12 — condition 3.

Key points: paralleling a generator

Four conditions before closing

  1. Equal rms voltages (set with the field current).
  2. Same phase sequence (lamps together, not taking turns).
  3. Phase angles equal: Δθ ≈ 0 (lamps dark, pointer at 12).
  4. Generator frequency slightly above the bus (pointer turns slowly clockwise).

Five steps

  1. Bring the machine to speed, field off.
  2. Raise IF until Vg = Vbus.
  3. Check the sequence with the three lamps.
  4. Set fg slightly above fbus.
  5. Close when the lamps are dark.

What the lecture showed

  1. Several generators share one varying load; units come on and off the bus.
  2. Close only when all four conditions hold.
  3. Unequal voltages: lamps never fully dark; closing drives a reactive circulating current.
  4. Swapping any two phases reverses the sequence (and a motor's direction).
  5. Lamps together: right sequence. Lamps take turns: wrong.
  6. Wrong sequence: closing is a short circuit — √3 V across two poles.
  7. Out of phase: surge and torque shock, worst at 180° (ΔV = 2V).
  8. Frequency: far off — no pull-in; slightly low — it motors; slightly high (0.05–0.2 Hz) — it generates.
  9. All four met: it joins smoothly and picks up a little power, P = sP(fnl − fsys).

Tutorial: the five-step procedure

Each step sets up the Lab for you. Press ▶ Set up, then do and look where the step says.

Step 1

Bring the machine up to speed with the field off.

Do: press ⏵ Run up to speed. Look at: fg reads about 60.30 Hz, the generator voltmeter reads 0 kV, and all three lamps glow steadily — each sees the full bus phase voltage.

Step 2

Raise the field current until Vg = Vbus.

Do: press ⚡ Excite field (about 12.8 kV), then the field +0.1 kV and +0.01 buttons until Vg reads 13.80 kV. Look at: checklist line 1 turns ✓.

Step 3

Check the phase sequence with the three lamps.

Look at: lamps A, B and C light one after another — the sequence is wrong. Press ABC (swap two leads back): now all three brighten and go dark together.

Step 4

Set fg slightly above fbus.

Do: press governor −0.1 Hz twice and −0.01 five times, to 60.05 Hz. Look at: the synchroscope slows to one clockwise turn every 1/0.05 = 20 s, and the generator wave in the waveform panel drifts slowly ahead of the bus wave.

Step 5

Close when the lamps are dark and the pointer is at 12 o'clock.

Do: wait for the Ready light, then press Close breaker. Look at: the blades close, the surge meter barely moves, the tag reads IN STEP, and the machine picks up a little power.

Reading the lamps

The lamps blink at the slip frequency |fg − fbus|: 0.5 Hz difference, one blink every 2 s. How bright they are at any instant is set by the phase difference: Vlamp = 2V sin(Δθ/2), dark at Δθ = 0, brightest at 180°.

All three together = same phase sequence. One after another (rotating) = wrong sequence; they are never all dark at once.

The waveform panel shows the same thing as waves: the generator wave slides past the bus wave once every 1/slip seconds, and their difference (the lamp voltage) swells and shrinks — a beat at the slip frequency.

Chapman's four conditions are: equal rms voltages, the same phase sequence, equal phase angles, and the incoming frequency slightly higher than the running system's.

Experiments

Five short investigations, about 30 minutes. Each ▶ Set up puts the Lab in the right state. Predict before you look.

Experiment 1

Wrong phase sequence

Goal: see what the lamps do when two leads are swapped, and fix it.

  1. Predict: will lamps A, B, C blink together or one after another?
  2. Watch for 10 s. Then press ABC.
  1. Prediction, then what you saw with ACB.
  2. Why are the lamps never all dark at the same time with ACB?
What you should see

With ACB the lamps take turns — the dark lamp moves A → C → B (or the reverse) and never all three go dark together, because phases B and C of the generator sit 120° away from the bus phases they face. Pressing ABC makes all three brighten and go dark together.

Experiment 4

Unequal voltages

Goal: see why the lamps never go fully dark when Vg ≠ Vbus, then close anyway.

  1. The set-up puts the field low on purpose (Vg = 8.29 kV). Watch the lamps at their darkest; read lamp A's voltage then.
  2. Close when the lamps are darkest. Read the surge.
  1. Why do the lamps never go fully dark?
  2. What surge did you get, and which condition was missing?
What you should see

At Δθ = 0 the lamp still reads |Vbus − Vg| = 7.97 − 4.78 = 3.18 kV per phase, so the lamps stay dimly lit. Closing at Δθ ≈ 0 gives about 418 A (0.40 × rated): it joins with a jolt (amber tag). Condition 1, equal voltages, was missing.

Experiment 5

Close with the generator slow

Goal: find which way power flows after closing with fg below the bus.

  1. Watch the synchroscope direction. Close when the lamps are dark.
  1. Predict first: generating or motoring? Then read P after the close.
What you should see

The pointer turns counter-clockwise (SLOW). After the close the machine is pulled up to 60.00 Hz and runs as a motor: P = 10.4 × (59.90 − 60.00) ≈ −1.04 MW. That is why condition 4 asks for fg slightly above the bus.
