Two Generators Sharing Real and Reactive Power

ECE 3033 · Lecture 5 · v1.0.1
Model

Two identical three-phase, Y-connected units: 4 MVA, 4.16 kV, Vφ = 2,401.8 V, rated IA = 555.1 A, ZS = 0.2 + j4.5 Ω per phase. Load on slides 1 to 4: 5.0 MW at 0.80 lagging, so Qload = 3.75 MVAR and IL = 867.4∠−36.87° A. Slide 5 has its own load, 5.6 MW at 0.85 lagging.

Governors (slides 1 and 2): straight droop lines with sP = 2.2514 MW/Hz (3 % speed droop at fnl = 61.0 Hz, 4 MW full load). Pk = sP(fnl,k − fsys) and P1 + P2 = Pload. Moving a set point slides the line parallel to itself; sP does not change.

Fields (slides 3 to 5): the terminal voltage is held at 4.16 kV and taken as the reference. Each unit's current is IAk = (Pk − jQk) / 3Vφ, the currents add to IL, and EAk = Vφ + IAkZS. No saturation; the field current is not computed, only the EA it must produce.

Steady state only: no governor, rotor or voltage-regulator dynamics, no losses other than RA in EA. In the phasor diagrams the currents are drawn on their own scale: directions are exact, lengths are not comparable with the voltages.

1 of 5 · Two units, one load

Two units, one load: only the totals are fixed.

No bus holds the frequency; it settles where the two shares add up to the load.

The two frequency axes stand one load apart, like the walls of a house. G1's droop line starts at the left wall, G2's at the right wall, drawn mirrored. Where they cross, both units are at the same frequency and their powers fill the floor exactly.

The reactive power is shared the same way, but by the fields, not the governors: slides 3 and 4.

2 of 5 · Moving the set points

Move one set point and both the split and the frequency move.

To change the split and keep 60 Hz, move both set points.

Raising one set point slides that unit's line up. The crossing moves toward the other wall, so the unit takes more of the load, and it moves up, so the frequency rises.

Raise one set point and lower the other by the right amounts and the crossing moves sideways only: a new split at the same frequency.

3 of 5 · Field current shares Q

The governors set P; the fields set Q.

Raise one field and lower the other: Q moves between units while the voltage holds.

The load decides the total, 3.75 MVAR. The fields decide who supplies it. The unit that takes more Q needs a longer EA, which means more field current.

Raising one field alone would also change the terminal voltage; moving both together holds it.

4 of 5 · Currents and internal voltages

Currents add by KCL; each unit's EA follows its own current.

The unit carrying more Q needs the larger EA: more field current.

Set G1's power and power factor. The load current is fixed by the load, so G2's current is whatever is left: IA2 = IL − IA1.
