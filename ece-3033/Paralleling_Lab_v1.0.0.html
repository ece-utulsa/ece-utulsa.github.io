Key points: paralleling a generator

Four conditions before closing

  1. Equal rms voltages (set with the field current).
  2. Same phase sequence (lamps together, not taking turns).
  3. Phase angles equal: Δθ ≈ 0 (lamps dark, pointer at 12).
  4. Generator frequency slightly above the bus (pointer turns slowly clockwise).

Five steps

  1. Bring the machine to speed, field off.
  2. Raise IF until Vg = Vbus.
  3. Check the sequence with the three lamps.
  4. Set fg slightly above fbus.
  5. Close when the lamps are dark.

Tutorial: the five-step procedure

Each step sets up the Lab for you. Press ▶ Set up, then look where the step says.

Step 1

Bring the machine up to speed with the field off.

Look at: the generator voltmeter reads 0 kV, and all three lamps glow steadily — each sees the full bus phase voltage.

Step 2

Raise the field current until Vg = Vbus.

Look at: drag IF up to 5.00 A and watch the generator voltmeter reach 13.80 kV. Checklist line 1 turns ✓.

Step 3

Check the phase sequence with the three lamps.

Look at: the lamps light one after another — the sequence is wrong. Press ABC (swap two leads back): now all three brighten and darken together.

Step 4

Set fg slightly above fbus.

Look at: the synchroscope turns slowly clockwise (FAST), one turn every 1/0.05 = 20 s, and the lamps blink once every 20 s.

Step 5

Close when the lamps are dark and the pointer is at 12 o'clock.

Look at: wait for the Ready light, then press Close breaker. The machine joins smoothly and picks up a little power.

Reading the lamps

The lamps blink at the slip frequency |fg − fbus|: 0.5 Hz difference, one blink every 2 s. How bright they are at any instant is set by the phase difference: Vlamp = 2V sin(Δθ/2), dark at Δθ = 0, brightest at 180°.

All three together = same phase sequence. One after another (rotating) = wrong sequence; they are never all dark at once.

Chapman's four conditions are: equal rms voltages, the same phase sequence, equal phase angles, and the incoming frequency slightly higher than the running system's.

Experiments

Five short investigations, about 30 minutes. Each ▶ Set up puts the Lab in the right state. Predict before you look.

Experiment 1

Wrong phase sequence

Goal: see what the lamps do when two leads are swapped, and fix it.

  1. Predict: will the lamps blink together or one after another?
  2. Watch for 10 s. Then press ABC.
  1. Prediction, then what you saw with ACB.
  2. Why are the lamps never all dark at the same time with ACB?
What you should see

With ACB the lamps take turns — the dark spot rotates A → C → B (or the reverse) and never all three go dark together, because phases B and C of the generator sit 120° away from the bus phases they face. Pressing ABC makes all three brighten and darken together.

What you should see

90°: |ΔV| = 11.27 kV, surge ≈ 1479 A (1.41 × rated) — large surge, trip. 180°: 15.93 kV, ≈ 2092 A (2.0 × rated) — trip. 5°: 0.70 kV, ≈ 91 A (0.09 × rated) — smooth. Equal frequencies here, so the 5° close floats at P ≈ 0.

Experiment 4

Unequal voltages

Goal: see why the lamps never go fully dark when Vg ≠ Vbus, then close anyway.

  1. Watch the lamps at their darkest. Read lamp A's voltage at that moment.
  2. Close when the lamps are darkest. Read the surge.
  1. Why do the lamps never go fully dark?
  2. What surge did you get, and which condition was missing?
What you should see

Vg = 10.49 kV against 13.80 kV. At Δθ = 0 the lamp still reads |Vbus − Vg| = 7.97 − 6.06 = 1.91 kV per phase, so it never goes dark. Closing at Δθ ≈ 0 gives about 250 A (0.24 × rated): it joins with a jolt. Condition 1, equal voltages, was missing.

Experiment 5

Close with the generator slow

Goal: find which way power flows after closing with fg below the bus.

  1. Watch the synchroscope direction. Close when the lamps are dark.
  1. Predict first: generating or motoring? Then read P after the close.
What you should see

The pointer turns counter-clockwise (SLOW). After the close the machine is pulled up to 60.00 Hz and runs as a motor: P = 10.4 × (59.90 − 60.00) ≈ −1.04 MW. That is why condition 4 asks for fg slightly above the bus.
