Static Stability Limit

ECE 3033 · Lecture 2 · v1.1.0
Model

Machine G3: 25 MVA, 13.8 kV, 4 poles, 1800 r/min (ωm = 188.5 rad/s), XS = 7.62 Ω (1.0 pu), on the infinite bus: Vφ = 7.97 kV, 60 Hz. EA = Vφ × IF / 5.00 A (no saturation).

Slide 1 uses illustrative losses: friction and windage 0.15 MW, core 0.20 MW, stray 0.01 × Pout, copper 3IA²RA with RA = 0.07 Ω. IA comes from the lossless phasor relation at the set field current. Its band and arrow widths are not to scale. The Lab's power-flow panel uses the same losses at unity power factor, with widths that follow MW.

From slide 2 on RA and the rotational losses are ignored (RA ≪ XS), so the input power equals Pconv = Pout = 3VφEA sin δ / XS. Each lecture diagram has one fixed scale. IA is drawn for direction only: its length is not comparable with the voltages.

The loss of synchronism is qualitative: when the input power exceeds Pmax the rotor angle is run away at a rate that grows with (Pin − Pmax sin δ), slowed so it can be followed, and the breaker trips after one pole slip. Transient stability (swings after a sudden change) is not modelled. δ moves to a new steady value with a short display lag.

1 of 6 · Where the power goes

Not all the input power reaches the bus

Losses come out before and after the conversion to electrical power. What is left goes to the infinite bus.

Infinite bus: a power system so large that nothing G3 does changes its voltage or frequency: Vφ = 7.97 kV at 60 Hz.

2 of 6 · The phasor diagram

More power swings EA further ahead of Vφ

The field is fixed, so |EA| is fixed. More power raises the torque angle δ, and the vertical EA sin δ grows with it.

δ: torque angle, EA from Vφ. θ: power factor angle, IA from Vφ. RA ignored from here on.

3 of 6 · From the diagram to Pmax

Power depends on δ, and it has a peak

The vertical EA sin δ in the phasor diagram is the power, scaled. It cannot be longer than EA, so P has a peak at δ = 90°.

Step through the derivation under the diagrams. The sliders unlock at step 6.

4 of 6 · Static stability limit

Past the peak, no torque angle balances the input power

G3 runs where the input power line crosses the curve. Above Pmax there is no crossing: G3 slips poles and the breaker trips. More field current lifts the curve.

Qualitative: the pole slip is illustrated, not computed. Transient stability is not modelled.

Four short investigations, about 30 minutes. Each ▶ Set up puts the Lab in the right state. Predict before you look.

Experiment 1

Losses and load

Goal: see which loss grows with load. Use the power-flow panel (illustrative losses, unity power factor).

  1. Set the input power to 5, 15 and 25 MW in turn. Record Pout and the efficiency.
Pin (MW)Pout (MW)Efficiency (%)
5.0
15.0
25.0
  1. Predict first: which loss grows fastest with load, and why? Then say what you saw.
What you should see

Pout ≈ 4.60, 14.43 and 24.19 MW; efficiency ≈ 91.9, 96.2 and 96.8 %. Friction and windage and core loss stay at 0.15 and 0.20 MW. The copper loss 3IA²RA grows as the square of the current: about 0.008, 0.077 and 0.215 MW. The fixed losses weigh most at light load, so the efficiency is lowest there.

Experiment 2

Torque angle and power factor against power

Goal: at a fixed field, follow δ and the power factor as the load rises, and find the unity power factor load.

  1. The field stays at 6.00 A. Raise the input power in steps of 5 MW to 25 MW; read δ and the power factor each time.
  2. Find the load at which the power factor is unity.
  1. Predict first: does the power factor become more lagging or less as the load rises? Then give δ and pf at 5 and 25 MW.
  2. The unity power factor load and its δ.
What you should see

δ grows from 9.6° at 5 MW to 56.4° at 25 MW. G3 is lagging (supplying reactive power) at light load, unity at about 16.6 MW (δ = 33.6°, where EA cos δ = Vφ), and leading above it. The field did not change; only the power did.

  2. Plot Pmax against IF on paper.

What you should see

EA = 7.97, 9.56, 11.15 and 12.75 kV; Pmax = 25.0, 30.0, 35.0 and 40.0 MW. A straight line through the origin: Pmax = 3VφEA/XS is proportional to EA, and EA to IF (no saturation in this model).

Experiment 4

Margin

Goal: find the field current that carries 20 MW at δ = 25°.

  1. The input power stays at 20 MW. Raise the field until δ reads 25°.
  1. Predict first with P = Pmax sin δ: what Pmax is needed? Then give the IF you found and the margin in MW.
What you should see

At 6.00 A, 20 MW sits at δ = 41.8°. δ = 25° needs Pmax = 20 / sin 25° = 47.3 MW, which is IF ≈ 9.46 A (9.45 A gives 25.0°). The margin is then about 27 MW.
