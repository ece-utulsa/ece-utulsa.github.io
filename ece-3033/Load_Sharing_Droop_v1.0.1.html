Load Sharing and Speed Droop

ECE 3033 · Lecture 4 · v1.0.1
Model

Three 100 MW units with 4 poles (shaft speed in r/min = 30 × frequency in Hz). G1: fnl = 61.0 Hz, SD = 3.0 %. G2: 61.5 Hz, 4.0 %. G3: 60.8 Hz, 2.5 %.

Each governor follows a straight droop line: ffl = fnl / (1 + SD), sP = Pfl / (fnl − ffl) with Pfl = 100 MW, and P = sP(fnl − fsys). Units sharing a load run at one frequency, fsys = (Σ sPk fnl,k − Pload) / Σ sPk. On the infinite bus fsys = 60.00 Hz whatever the unit does.

Moving a set point (slides 2 and 4) slides the droop line parallel to itself: the unit keeps the slope sP it has at its starting set point. On slide 1 the slope follows the fnl and SD sliders.

Steady state only: the frequency and the powers jump to their new values; governor and rotor dynamics are not modelled. The lines are drawn past 100 MW and below 0 MW to show the trend; a unit there is flagged (above its rating, or motoring on reverse power). Losses and reactive power are left out.

1 of 5 · Speed droop

More load, lower speed: the governor droops on purpose.

A straight line from fnl to ffl sets how much power the unit gives at each frequency.

What sets the power. The unit must supply the whole load: P = Pload. The governor has no target in MW; it senses only speed. When the load rises, the shaft slows and the governor opens the fuel or steam valve in proportion to the drop. The droop line is that proportion.

What the droop decides. The slope sP = Pfl/(fnl − ffl) is how many MW the unit gives for each hertz the frequency falls. Small SD → flat line, large sP: many MW per Hz, a stiff unit. Large SD → steep line, small sP.

Why it matters. Alone, the frequency falls along the line until the unit supplies the full load. In parallel every unit sees the same frequency, so each one's share is set by its own fnl and sP; when the load changes, the stiffer unit (smaller SD) takes the larger share.

2 of 5 · On the infinite bus

On the infinite bus the frequency is fixed; the set point decides the power.

Raise fnl and the unit takes more load; the bus supplies the rest.

The bus holds 60.00 Hz whatever G1 does. G1's power is where its droop line crosses that frequency. Raising the set point slides the line up and the crossing to the right.

The area load does not change G1's power: the bus takes up the difference, Pbus = Pload − PG1.

3 of 5 · Sharing a load

One frequency, three droop lines: each line sets that unit's share.

The frequency settles where the units together supply the whole load.

No bus holds the frequency here. All three units turn at the same electrical speed, so one horizontal line cuts all three droop lines; each crossing is that unit's power.

4 of 5 · Moving a set point

Raise one set point: that unit takes more, the others less, and the frequency rises.

Moving set points is how the operators bring the frequency back to 60 Hz.

The load has not changed, so what one unit picks up the others give back. The faint dashed line is where the moved unit's line was.
