Voltage Regulation

Model

Three-phase, Y-connected synchronous generator: 2.5 MVA, 4160 V, 60 Hz, RA = 0.12 Ω and XS = 6.2 Ω per phase. Full load: |Vφ| = 2,402 V and IA = 347.0 A at rated S.

EA = Vφ + RAIA + jXSIA, with Vφ as the reference. The field current and the speed are fixed at the full-load setting for the selected power factor, so |EA| does not change when the load is removed. At no load IA = 0 and Vφ = EA.

VR = (|EA| − |Vφ|) / |Vφ| × 100 %, with the full-load |Vφ|. First-order model: no voltage regulator, no saturation, steady state only. The phasor diagrams are to scale. The inset on slide 1 is the same diagram enlarged 10 times about the tip of Vφ; the current dial on slide 2 shows the direction of IA only.

1 of 3 · Voltage regulation

What voltage regulation measures

The voltage regulation of a synchronous generator is the rise in terminal voltage when full rated load at a specified power factor is removed, with rotor speed and field current held constant, as a percentage of the full-load voltage.

At no load IA = 0, so Vnl = |EA|. At full load Vfl = |Vφ|.

Find EA with phasors, magnitude and angle. VR then uses magnitudes only: δ drops out.

VR is not the length of the drop: |jXSIA| = V at every power factor.

2 of 3 · Power factor

Power factor decides the voltage regulation

Same load S, same |Vφ|. A lagging load needs a longer EA; a leading load can need a shorter one, and VR turns negative. At each power factor the field is readjusted to hold |Vφ| = 2,402 V at full load.

jXSIA is always 90° ahead of IA (dial). Lagging IA points the drop up and outward, lengthening EA. Leading IA tips it back, shortening EA.

|IA| is fixed, so the tip of EA stays on a circle about the tip of Vφ. Outside the dashed |Vφ| arc: VR > 0. Inside: VR < 0.
