Static Stability Limit

Model

Machine G3: 25 MVA, 13.8 kV, 4 poles, 1800 r/min (ωm = 188.5 rad/s), XS = 7.62 Ω (1.0 pu), on the infinite bus: Vφ = 7.97 kV, 60 Hz. EA = Vφ × IF / 5.00 A (no saturation).

Slide 2 uses illustrative losses: friction and windage 150.0 kW, core 200.0 kW, stray 0.01 × Pout, copper 3IA²RA with RA = 0.07 Ω. IA comes from the lossless phasor relation at the set field current. Its band and arrow widths are not to scale. The Lab's power-flow panel uses the same losses at unity power factor, with widths that follow MW.

On every other slide RA and the rotational losses are ignored (RA ≪ XS), so the input power equals Pconv = Pout = 3VφEA sin δ / XS. Each lecture diagram has one fixed scale. On slide 3 IA is drawn on its own scale: its length is not comparable with the voltages.

The loss of synchronism (slide 1 and the Lab) is qualitative: when the input power exceeds Pmax the rotor angle is run away at a rate that grows with (Pin − Pmax sin δ), slowed so it can be followed, and the breaker trips after one pole slip. Transient stability (swings after a sudden change) is not modelled. δ moves to a new steady value with a short display lag. The 25 MW rating is thermal; powers above it are shown to see the trend.

1 of 7 · The static stability limit

How much power can G3 send to the bus?

The static stability limit is the largest real power a synchronous generator can deliver to the bus without losing synchronism, when the load changes slowly.

Higher EA (more field) raises it; higher XS lowers it. Past δ = 90° the power falls as δ grows, so G3 cannot hold step.

You will: find Pmax from the phasor diagram; read δ and the margin off the curve; set the field for a required margin.

2 of 7 · Where the power goes

Losses are small: Pin ≈ Pout

About 96 % reaches the bus, so from here on RA and the rotational losses are ignored.

Infinite bus: a power system so large that nothing G3 does changes its voltage or frequency: Vφ = 7.97 kV at 60 Hz.

3 of 7 · The phasor diagram

Why EA sin δ = XS IA cos θ

The vertical from the tip of EA is a side of two right triangles. Read it in each one: the two results are the same line, so they are equal.

δ: torque angle, EA from Vφ. θ: power factor angle, IA from Vφ. Step through the proof under the diagram.

4 of 7 · From the diagram to Pmax

More power, fixed field

The field is fixed, so |EA| is fixed and its tip stays on the arc. More power raises δ and the height EA sin δ, which is the power, scaled. It cannot be longer than EA, so P peaks at δ = 90°.

5 of 7 · Raising the limit

More field, fixed power

P is fixed at 20 MW, so EA sin δ is fixed: the tip of EA slides along the horizontal line. More field lifts Pmax and lowers δ.

Left of the unity-pf line G3 is leading (it absorbs reactive power); right of it, lagging (it supplies reactive power).

What you should see

Pout ≈ 4.6, 14.4 and 24.2 MW; efficiency ≈ 91.9, 96.2 and 96.8 %. Friction and windage and core loss stay at 150.0 and 200.0 kW. The copper loss 3IA²RA grows as the square of the current: about 7.8, 76.5 and 215.1 kW. The fixed losses weigh most at light load, so the efficiency is lowest there.

Experiment 2

Torque angle and power factor against power

Goal: at a fixed field, follow δ and the power factor as the load rises, and find the unity power factor load.

  1. The field stays at 6.00 A. Raise the input power in steps of 5 MW to 25 MW; read δ and the power factor each time.
  2. Find the load at which the power factor is unity.
  1. Predict first: does the power factor become more lagging or less as the load rises? Then give δ and pf at 5 and 25 MW.
  2. The unity power factor load and its δ.
What you should see

δ grows from 9.6° at 5 MW to 56.4° at 25 MW. G3 is lagging (supplying reactive power) at light load, unity at about 16.6 MW (δ = 33.6°, where EA cos δ = Vφ), and leading above it. The field did not change; only the power did.

What you should see

Experiment 4

Margin

Goal: find the field current that carries 20 MW at δ = 25°.

  1. The input power stays at 20 MW. Raise the field until δ reads 25°.
  1. Predict first with P = Pmax sin δ: what Pmax is needed? Then give the IF you found and the margin in MW.
What you should see

At 6.00 A, 20 MW sits at δ = 41.8°. δ = 25° needs Pmax = 20 / sin 25° = 47.3 MW, which is IF ≈ 9.46 A (9.45 A gives 25.0°). The margin is then about 27.3 MW.
